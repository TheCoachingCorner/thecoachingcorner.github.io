Slow Them Down

What we tell the players

PRESSURE → TURN THEM → RUN TOGETHER

Coaches: this is close, contain, capitalize. Never say those words to the kids.

PRESSURE

Close the space fast — then stop before you reach them.

The point“Fast to get there. But stop short.”

If we stop short, that gives us space and time to keep up and force a mistake.

The gap“Stay one big step away.”

Close enough to touch them — but don’t. Too far and they go by you; too close and one touch beats you.

TURN THEM

Block off one path. Give them only one way to go.

The feet“Scooter stance.”

One foot in front, one behind. Show them only one shoulder.

Where to look“Watch their belly button, not the ball.”

Feet can trick you. Their hips can’t lie.

Which way“Send them to the sideline.”

Stand so one way is easy and one way is hard, and the easy way is always away from the middle. Same thing their position card says: keep the ball away from the center.

Careful“You turn them by standing in the way. Not by running through them.”

If they stop, turn, or go backwards — that’s the whole job done. You already won.

RUN TOGETHER

Now you’re going down the field side by side. Stay with them for as long as it takes — and when the ball comes loose, take it and go.

The whole idea“Don’t kick it away. Dribble it away.”

This is the one that kills stabbing, and it does it without a single “don’t”: you cannot dribble away from a stab. A poke sends the ball somewhere random. To come away dribbling you have to arrive under control — which only happens if you waited until the ball was actually loose.

If it never comes“No ball? You still won.”

This one is a bonus, not the job. If you ran with them the whole way and they never got past you, that is a complete piece of defending — whoever ends up with the ball.

When it does come“Wait for them to lose it. Then it’s yours.”

Their position card says the same thing: control the ball — kicking away is a pass to the other team. If you reach, they go past you.

Midfield — stay behind the ball

Imagine a line drawn across the field through the ball. Everyone except the forward wants to be on our side of that line.

Getting back“Beat the ball home.”
How hard to run“Run to where the ball is going, not where it is.”

If you’ll get there either way — walk. If you won’t — run.

Exercise — The 6-Second Wall

Whole team

Set up 3 or 4 channels side by side, about 12 yds long and 6 yds wide. One attacker with a ball at one end, one defender at the other. Everyone is in a channel — nobody watching.

The defender’s job is not to win the ball. It’s to stop the attacker crossing the end line for 6 seconds. Count it out loud.

  • Attacker crosses the line → attacker gets the point
  • Defender survives 6 seconds → defender gets the point

Round 1 — walking only, no ball.

This is the round that teaches it. A walking defender cannot stab, so the only thing that works is arriving on a curve, getting sideways, and staying in front.

Round 2 — defender may jog. Round 3 — live.

Swap attacker and defender every rep. Play to 3 points, then find a new partner.

“Turn them!”“One big step away!”“Scooter feet!”“Run together!”
2

Defenders Help Each Other

What we tell the players

The two defenders are partners. This section is about which one goes — what that one does when they get there is pressure → turn them → run together, from section 1.

The cue“ONE PRESSURES — ONE BACKS UP.”
Pressure — closest to the ball“Go slow them down.”
Backup — the other one“Get behind your partner and protect the goal.”

If they get past your partner, you’re there.

Exercise — Partners at the Gate

Adult #1Adult #2

Two stations, one adult each. Split the team in half.

Each station: a 3-yd gate made of two cones. Two defenders protect it. Everyone else is an attacker with a ball, spread out about 15 yds away.

Attackers go one at a time, every 8 seconds — try to dribble through the gate. Waiting attackers keep dribbling in their own space. Nobody stands still.

Who pressures? Who backs up?

Don’t worry yet about whether the attacker scores. Stop for five seconds and praise the shape: “Perfect — you went, and you were behind helping.”

Swap rule, say it once: “After four attackers, the two defenders join the attackers and the next two become defenders.”

Progression: attackers go in pairs — now it’s 2v2 and the backup defender has a real decision to make.

“Who’s pressuring?”“Get behind your partner!”“Don’t stand beside each other!”
3

How to Chase Someone Down

What we tell the players

The best time to win the ball back is right away — most players slow down as soon as they get it.

First“Win it back in three seconds.”
If you didn’t“Run to where they’re going, not where they are.”

Cut the corner. Run alongside the ball, not behind the player.

Getting past them“Get your shoulder in front of theirs.”

Swim your arm through and take the space — now they’re running behind you instead of past you.

The line refs call“Shoulder to shoulder is soccer. Hands are a foul.”

Shoulder next to shoulder is legal. An arm across their body, a pull, or a push in the back is not.

Don’t chase someone for 40 yards when you’re never going to catch them. Turn around and get back to your position instead.

Exercise — Shoulder to Shoulder

Whole team

Pairs, side by side on a line. A cone 12 yds away. Coach rolls a ball toward the cone; both players go.

Round 1 — walking only, friendly.

Both players get there. Practice the shoulder and the swim on the way. Nobody wins.

Round 2 — walking only, competitive. First to the ball. Round 3 — running, competitive.

One rule the whole time: “Shoulder to shoulder. No hands.” Hands means the other player gets the point.

Then move the cone off to one side so they have to cut the angle to get there first.

“Cut the corner!”“Shoulder, not hands!”“Three seconds — go get it!”

Scrimmage

Finish with as much 7v7-style soccer as our numbers allow. Don’t constantly stop play. Three things to watch for:

A defender stabs and gets beatenFreeze it. Replay it.

Back them up five steps and run it again with tiny steps. Takes 20 seconds.

Both defenders go to the ball“Who’s backing up?”
We lose the ball“One — two — three!”

Count out loud so they hear the window to win it back.

Praise the backup defender who never touched the ball. Loudly, by name. That job is invisible and it dies without it.
