Ends at 60 minutes

Never yell “SHOOT!” It makes them rush and blast. The word all hour is “Pass it in!” — the word you use is the shot you get.

Power Shot Basics ends in Lightning, so they are one block and there is no break between them — the kicking turns into shooting without stopping. What did not fit is Extra Time at the bottom.

Key Concepts

Three things to say all hour. They are not the lesson — they are what to fall back on when a drill starts falling apart.

Every shot, all session“Pass it in.”

A shot is a pass into the goal. Say it in the warm-up, say it in every drill, say it in the scrimmage — and never say the other word.

Before the pass, the shot and the run“Look first.”

Cross-Fire is built entirely on this, and it is what stops a shooter hitting it at the goalie. Not “head up” this session — half of today is telling them to put their head down and watch the ball, and the two cues fight each other. Look first, then the head goes wherever the job needs it. Other sessions can go back to head up; this one should not.

The shape of the session

Everybody finishes. Every drill today ends with a ball going at a goal, including the warm-up circle if there is a minute spare. Nobody should leave without having hit one.

1

Warm-up — Ball Work

Three minutes, a ball each, from the minute they arrive. No lines, and nothing a late arrival needs explained to them — Cross-Fire is the real warm-up and it starts two minutes later.

  • Touches — both feet, tap the ball side to side.
  • Drag backs — sole of the foot, pull it back, turn, go the other way.
  • Zig-zag dribbling — through a line of cones and back.

Last minute: “Look first!” while they dribble. It is the first of the day’s two words and it costs nothing to start here.

2

Drill — Cross-Fire

Drill · Cross-Fire 7 minutes Reinforces · lesson 10

Overview

Full rules in the drill
  1. Setup — a ring of cones 12 to 15 yards across, three more cones than players, one player on a cone.
  2. Pass across. Never to your neighbour. After you pass, go to an open cone.
  3. One ball, then add the movement, then two balls.
  4. “How many passes in thirty seconds?” Count out loud, all together, then try to beat it.

If it is flying, finish with monkey in the middle — one player in, and whoever loses the ball takes their place. Do not let it eat the clock.

3

Lesson — Shooting Mechanics

Lesson · 14 — Shooting Mechanics 2 minutes

Two minutes, and only the framing. The drill that follows teaches the four parts one round at a time — do not teach them here as well.

Reinforce these two today

Two kinds of shots“Accuracy first. Power second.”

Close in, it is a pass into the goal with the side of your foot. Far out, it is the laces. Avoid 100% power — placement, then technique, then power.

Which foot to use, in five words“Laces for power. Arch for sure.”

Want it hard? Hit it with your shoelaces — the hard bone on top of your foot. Want it where you aimed? Use the arch, the inside of your foot, the same one you pass with. That is the whole introduction; everything else arrives in the drill, one round at a time.

4

Drill — Power Shot Basics → Lightning

Drill · Power Shot Basics, finishing with Lightning 11 + 5 minutes Reinforces · lesson 14

Overview

Full rules in the drill
  1. Setup — two cone lines about half a field apart, a ball each, five big steps between players. A back line five steps behind the start, for anyone who crosses in two kicks.
  2. Nobody kicks until the call. Nobody walks until the call.
  3. A round is one crossing. Kick, walk to your ball, kick again, until you are over.
  4. Round 1 hammer foot, half power. Round 2 plant foot, three-quarter. Round 3 swing through, full power — and count your kicks.
Finish

Lightning · 5 minutes

Straight into Lightning with no break — this is where the kicking turns into shooting.

  1. One round only. Cone in the centre, first to five.
  2. Shoot, then run into goal. The old goalie fetches and takes the ball back to the line.
  3. Keep your own score and yell it. Goal is a point, save is a point.

Everything from the crossings is in it — laces, hard foot, plant foot, swing through — now at a goal with somebody standing in it.

Eleven minutes is tight for three crossings. If they need a fourth or the explaining runs long, take it out of the scrimmage — never out of Lightning, which is the part they will remember.

5

Drill — Pass Out, Pass It In, Shoot

Drill · Pass Out, Pass It In, Shoot 8 minutes Reinforces · lessons 14, 10

Overview

Full rules in the drill
  1. Setup — two mirrored stations at one goal, five a side. Balls beside each post, a cone out wide level with the top of the big box.
  2. Pass it out to the wide player. Pass it in across the face of the goal. Shoot — first time if they can.
  3. The two sides alternate. Left, then right — one ball live at a time.
  4. Rotation: passer → wide → shooter → collect the balls.

The cue that matters is “In front of them!” — a pass that arrives but sits still is a bad pass here, even though it got there.

6

Scrimmage

Fifteen minutes — the biggest block of the session. Do not stop play to coach; say the cue and let it run.

“Pass it in!”“Look first!”
Somebody blasts it“Pass it in!”

Not shoot, ever — and not unlucky either, which teaches nothing.

A shot goes over the bar“Chest over it.”

That is the lean, and it is the single most useful correction anyone will get today.

Somebody dribbles until the chance is gone“One less dribble.”

The shot was there two touches ago. Say it once, by name, then let it go.

Praise the pass that set up the shot, louder than the shot. Nobody thanks the passer on their own, and this is the session where it matters.
