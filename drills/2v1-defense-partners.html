2v1 Defense Partners

Serves · Lesson 04 — Defense Partners 10 minutes four at a time

Needs · a goal, the big box, a ball, bibs. Same space as 2v2 to goal, so the two run back to back without moving a cone.

defense

The big idea

ONE GOES → ONE DROPS IN BEHIND

This is where the shape gets taught. 2v2 to goal is where they have to find it at a run.

1

Two Versions, In This Order

Version 1 teaches the shape. Version 2 takes it away. Do not start with version 2 — a child who has never held the shape cannot recover into it.

2

Version 1 — Already In Position

Everybody starts where they should be, so the only thing to learn is the movement.

ACentral
outside the box, with the ball
1Two big steps
in front of the attacker
2Ten feet behind
and slightly inside
How it runs

Walk it, then let it go

  1. Walk it. No tackling. The attacker walks at the pair. Defender 1 stops short and turns them. Defender 2 drops back as 1 goes forward, staying behind.
  2. Stop and fix it once. Whatever was wrong — square, too close, both going — say it, then walk it again.
  3. Jog it.
  4. Live.

Rotate every rep: attacker → presser → cover → off. When everyone has been through all three, the goalie swaps.

3

Version 2 — Both Square

Same drill, one change: the two defenders start level with each other, side by side. That is the mistake, set up on purpose.

The closest one goes.

4

Scoring

  • Attacker dribbles between the two defenders = 2 points. Whether they score or not.
  • Attacker scores any other way = 1 point.
  • Defenders win it and dribble out past the top of the box = 1 point.

Two points for the middle is the whole scoreboard doing the coaching: it prices the exact mistake, so the defenders feel being square before anybody explains it.

5

Modifiers

Easier
  • Walking only.
  • No goalie — the defenders win by just stopping the attacker for six seconds.
  • Mark the cover's spot with a cone the first few reps, then take it away.
Harder
  • The attacker starts with a running head start.
  • Defenders may not tackle at all — only stand in the way.
  • 2v2, which is 2v2 to goal.
6

What You Shout

“One goes, one covers!”“Behind your partner!” “Stop short!”“Wait for the mistake!”
7

What Did We Learn

At the water break, not on the grass.

  • How did you two decide who was going?
  • What happened the time you were both level?
  • Covering defender — how far back felt right?
  • Attacker — was it easier to go round them or between them?
  • Saturday: they have the ball and your partner goes to it. Where are you?
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

A 2v1 is the only place the shape is visible

In 2v2 there is a second attacker to worry about, and a defender who gets it wrong can blame the other one. With one attacker there is nothing else going on: either they were behind their partner or they were beside them, and the result says which.

Two points for the middle is the coaching

Lesson 04's line is it is easy to dribble between two people, and very hard to dribble past a person into another person. A scoreboard that pays double for the gap turns that sentence into something they feel on the second rep. Nobody has to be told they were square.

Version 1 before version 2

Holding a shape and recovering into one are different skills, and the second needs the first. A child who has never stood in the right place has nothing to recover to, and the drill just produces two children chasing.

Walk it first

Same reason as the 1v1 work: at a walk they can hear you, and the mistake is still a mistake at walking pace. At full speed the first rep is a collision and nobody learns anything.

The cover waits

The hardest thing to teach here is doing nothing — the covering defender's job is to be there, not to join in. Worth saying out loud that their best rep may involve never touching the ball.

Open Questions

  • Ten feet is lesson 04's number and it is untested on grass. Too close and the attacker beats both with one touch; too far and the cover never arrives.
  • Whether version 2 needs its own session. It might be better as the second time you run the drill rather than the back half of the first.
  • Whether the attacker should be allowed to shoot from distance. Right now they can, which rewards the defenders for closing down and might also just end reps early.
  • Four players is a station. With a full team that is two or three groups, which is two or three goals and adults — or it runs as one station while the rest are elsewhere.
