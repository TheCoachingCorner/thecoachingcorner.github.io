Power Shot Basics

Serves · Lesson 14 — Shooting Mechanics 15 minutes any number

Needs · a ball each, two cone lines far enough apart to take half a field apart, a third line five big steps behind the start, a goal for the Lightning finish

introshooting

The big idea

KICK → WALK → GET ACROSS

Everybody kicks at once, on the call. The walk to your ball is how you get it back.

1

Set Up

Two cone lines, about half a field apart — that is three to five kicks for most of them. The team spreads along one of them, one ball each, five big steps apart, all facing the same way.

The rule that makes it safe: nobody kicks until the call, nobody walks until the call. Everyone faces one way, so there is never anybody downfield.

The back line “Got across in two? You start from the back line.”

A third row of cones, five big steps behind the start. One or two of them will cross half a field in two kicks — they move themselves back, and nobody has to be told they are the strong kicker or left standing at the far line waiting for the rest.

2

How It Runs

Three rounds. A round is one crossing, and each one adds one thing to the kick and one notch of power.

  1. Explain the one new thing. Under a minute.
  2. Everyone kicks on the call. Nobody kicks between calls.
  3. “Go get it” — everyone walks to their own ball and waits there.
  4. Call again, and again, until the last player is over the far line.
  5. Turn around. The next round comes back the other way.
Round 1

Hammer foot · half power

  • Everybody press the hard bone above your big toe. That is what hits the ball.
  • Toes down, ankle locked. Hard foot.
  • Half power. You cannot feel where the ball hit your foot when you are swinging as hard as you can.
Round 2

Plant foot · three-quarter power

  • Other foot next to the ball, pointing where the ball is going.
  • Head down while you kick.
Round 3

Swing through · full power

  • Sandcastle right behind the ball. Kick the top off it.
  • Finish with your foot out in front. Let the kick pull you forward.
  • Now they may hit it as hard as they like.
  • Count your kicks on this one. That number is the one to beat next time.
Finish

Lightning · five minutes

The fun part, and where the kicking turns into shooting. Do not cut it for one more crossing.

  • Go straight into Lightning — one goal, two balls, shoot and then go in goal.
  • One round only, cone in the centre, first to five. The full three-round version with the cone moving across the field is its own drill.
  • Everything they just built is in it: laces, hard foot, plant foot, swing through — now at a goal with somebody in it.
3

Modifiers

Easier
  • Move the lines closer, so a crossing is two or three kicks.
  • Half power the whole way through.
  • No step into it — stand still and kick.
  • Two rounds instead of three. Round 3 keeps until next time.
Harder
  • Move the lines further apart, so a crossing is six or seven.
  • Two steps of run-up.
  • One touch to set the ball, then kick it. This is the real thing.
  • A round with the other foot.
4

What You Shout

“Half power!”“Toes down!” “Hard foot!”“Next to the ball!” “Head down!”“Kick through it!”
5

What Did We Learn

At the water break, not on the grass.

  • Which part of your foot are we kicking with? Show me where.
  • What happens when your foot goes floppy?
  • Where does your other foot go?
  • Which one of your kicks felt the best today — what did you do differently on that one?
  • On Saturday you get the ball a long way from their goal and nobody is near you. What are you doing?
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

The power comes last “Half power.”

Tell a nine-year-old they are doing power shots and the first kick is everything they have — at which point nothing can be felt and nothing can be corrected. Half speed in round 1 is not a warm-up; it is the only speed at which a kid notices where the ball met their foot. Same order as the lesson: placement, technique, power.

One new thing a round

Round 2 does not stop being about the hard foot. It just stops being the thing we are talking about. Five rounds for the lesson’s five parts would mean five explanations, and the rule is two minutes of talking a session.

Eyes on the ball is not a round

It cannot really be drilled — you watch who is looking up at the far line and shout “head down!”. A correction costs nothing to teach. A round costs a minute of standing still.

A crossing, not a count

It ends on its own, so nobody is counting for the coach. It gives every kid a score they keep themselves — how many kicks? — and a good strike is the only way to improve it. And it is a race against your own last number, not against whoever kicks furthest, so the child at the back still has something to win.

Only round 3 is counted

Rounds 1 and 2 are at half and three-quarter power on purpose, so their numbers mean nothing. Comparing them to round 3 would teach the wrong lesson — that swinging harder is what improved it.

Empty goal on purpose

A keeper makes them aim, and aiming is the other shot. Today is about hitting it cleanly.

Three formations were on the table. Shooting at the goal throughout gives the best feedback, but one goal cannot take ten kids kicking at once and ten balls behind the net is two minutes of fetching for thirty seconds of kicking. Pairs kicking to each other is fastest, but a ball hit with full laces at a nine-year-old is a ball they have to deal with, so it becomes a receiving drill. One line, everybody kicks, everybody walks: the walk is the retrieval, and it doubles as the rest that makes five hard kicks in a row survivable. The goal at the end buys back the feedback the lines cannot give.

Open Questions

  • Half a field is a guess. Three to five kicks is the rule it is trying to produce. Watch round 1 — if most are across in two at half power, move the far line back rather than sending everybody to the back line.
  • Whether one back line is enough. If the spread in the team is wide, the strongest may cross in two from the back line as well. A second back line is the obvious answer, and also five more cones and a thing to explain.
  • Whether a crossing is too many kicks. Watch round 3: if the last two kicks look worse than the first two, the lines are too far apart, not the coaching failing.
  • Everyone kicks on the call, but they end up at different distances. A player behind the pack is kicking toward players ahead of them in neighbouring lanes. Lanes five steps wide and a ball on the ground make that fine in theory. Watch it the first time.
  • Whether the goal finish wants a keeper once they can actually strike it. Not the first time through.
