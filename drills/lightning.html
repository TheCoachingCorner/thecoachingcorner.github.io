Lightning

Serves · Lesson 14 — Shooting Mechanics, Lesson 02 — Goalie Introduction 10 minutes 6–8 per goal

Needs · one goal, two balls, a cone for the shooting mark about 12 yards out, two cones for the way back

shootinggoalkeeping

The big idea

SHOOT → GO IN GOAL → GET THE BALL → BACK IN LINE

It never stops. The shooter is the next goalie, and the next shot is already on its way. One round of this is also the finish to Power Shot Basics.

1

Set Up

One goal, a shooting cone 12 yards out, the line behind it, and two cones marking the way back. Two balls — that is the whole engine.

The rule that makes it safe: come back the wide way, never across the shot.

2

How It Runs

  1. First player starts in goal. Everybody else is in the line.
  2. Next player shoots from the cone.
  3. The shooter runs straight into goal. They are the new goalie, immediately.
  4. The old goalie collects the ball and takes it back to the line, the wide way.
  5. The next shooter is already going. They do not wait for anybody.

It does not stop. By the time a shot is taken, the next ball is on the cone.

Scoring

Keep your own, out loud

  • Goal = 1 point.
  • Save = 1 point, to the goalie.
  • Miss = nothing, for anybody.
  • Yell your total every time it changes. “Four!” Nobody is checking, but everybody hears it.
  • Forgot your score? You are on zero.

First to five wins the round.

3

Three Rounds, Three Angles

Everyone back to zero, the cone moves, and go again. Same drill — what changes is where the goal is from where you are standing.

1Right side
far post is the left one
2Centre
both corners open
3Left side
far post is the right one
4

Modifiers

Easier
  • Move the cone in to eight yards.
  • No score at all for the first round — just the loop.
  • One ball, until the loop is understood. Slower, and that is fine the first time.
Harder
  • Move the cone back.
  • One touch to set it, then shoot. Two touches only.
  • The goalie has to throw the ball to the next shooter instead of carrying it.
  • Weak foot round.
5

What You Shout

“Pass it in!”“Which post?” “Far post!”“Be big!” “Next!”

“Far post” belongs to rounds 1 and 3. From the middle there are two of them.

6

What Did We Learn

At the water break, not on the grass.

  • Which was easier today, scoring or saving?
  • Where did the goals go in — high or low? Middle or corner?
  • The goalies who saved a lot — what were they doing?
  • What happened when somebody blasted it?
  • Which was easier, shooting from the side or from the middle? Why?
  • Saturday: you are one on one with their goalie. What are you doing with it?
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

Two balls is the engine

One ball means the whole group waits for a ball to be fetched, and a fetching queue is where a drill dies. With two, the collecting and the shooting happen at the same time and nobody is watching anybody.

Everybody keeps goal, every cycle

This is the only shape that gives ten children keeper reps in ten minutes. A goalkeeping drill with one goalie is a drill for one child and an audience of nine.

The score keeps itself

A number held in a head and yelled out is not refereed by anybody — and there is one of you and ten of them. Yelling it is the part that makes it work: a quiet score can be invented, a loud one gets corrected by the kid who was watching.

Three angles, not three of the same

The reset already exists, so moving the cone costs nothing and turns a repeat into a new problem. From the side, far post stops being a thing a coach says and becomes the only sensible answer — the near post is where the goalie already is. It is also the closest this drill gets to a real chance in a game, which almost never arrives straight on.

A save is worth the same as a goal

Keeping goal is not the punishment for having shot. It is the other half of the drill, worth the same point, which is also what stops the goalie being bored.

Open Questions

  • Five points a round is a guess. Ten was the first number and is probably right for a single round — but three rounds of ten is the whole practice. Watch how long round 1 takes and set the rest from it.
  • The cone order starts with the hardest angle. Centre is the easy one. Right, centre, left sweeps across the field, which is easy to see and easy to run; centre first would be the gentler teaching order. Untested either way.
  • A miss scores nothing, but a saved shot gives the goalie a point. On paper that makes missing better than testing the keeper. In practice they want to score — but watch for the child who works it out.
  • Two goals needs two adults, and that is the real limit on group size, not the drill. Six to eight per goal; past that the line is the problem.
