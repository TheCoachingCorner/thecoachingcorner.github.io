Shoulder Race

Serves · Lesson 03 — One on One Defense, the run together beat 6 minutes Any even number

Needs · a line on the field · 2 cones about 20 ft along it · a ball per pair from round three

introdefense

The big idea

WALK IT FIRST → THEN ADD SPEED

You cannot barge at walking pace, so the only thing that works is the shoulder.

Set Up

Two queues at one end of a line already on the field. The front pair both step onto the same line. Two cones about 20 feet along it make the finish.

Both of them are running down the one line. That is the whole design: two players and one path means somebody has to get their shoulder in, and “did you get in front” has an answer instead of an argument. Give them a lane each and there is nothing to contest.

How It Runs

Front pair steps onto the line, shoulder to shoulder. On the call, they both go down it. Next pair goes as soon as the last one crosses.

Four rounds

Whole team6 min
  1. Walk. No ball. Both on the line. Try to be the person in front at the finish.
  2. Stop and fix it. Whatever went wrong — leaning, hands, drifting off the line — say it once, then walk it again.
  3. Walk, with a ball. Now there is something to reach, not just a line to cross.
  4. Run, and swim. Get your body past theirs.

Modifiers

Easier
  • Shorter — about 10 feet. Over before anyone gets frustrated.
  • Walk every round. No running at all.
Harder
  • Start a step behind and catch up.
  • Finish cones off to one side, so they cut the angle as well.

What You Shout

“Shoulder, not hands!” “Swim in front!”
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

Why walking first “You cannot barge at walking pace.”

Take the speed away and the only things left are the ones being taught: the shoulder, staying on the line, getting half a body in front. Every round after is the same skill with more speed on top.

Round two is the drill “Stop. What went wrong?”

It is the round people skip, and the only one that changes anything. Round one exists to produce the mistake that round two fixes.

Open questions. 20 feet is a guess — long enough that getting in front is worth something, short enough that it does not become a sprint contest between the two fastest kids. — And round four is where an arm comes out. Whether that is a point for the other player or just a reset is worth deciding before it happens rather than during.
