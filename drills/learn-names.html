Learn Names

Serves · no lesson — it carries the encourage learning names key concept 5 minutes Any number

Needs · a ball each · one big box, with two ends marked if you want the race

introcommunicationdribbling

The big idea

HEAR A NAME → FIND THAT FACE

A name sticks when you have to pick the person out of a moving crowd.

Set Up

One box, big enough that nobody queues for space. A ball each. This one exists to be started in ten seconds.

How It Runs

The whole drill

Whole team5 min
  1. Players dribble anywhere in the box.
  2. The coach calls a name.
  3. Everybody dribbles to that player.

Call the next name before they arrive, so nobody stops moving.

Modifiers

Easier
  • No ball for the first round. Just run.
  • Point as you call, for the first few names, then stop pointing.
  • Call the quiet ones early and often. They are the point of the drill.
Harder
  • Call a name and shout “AWAY!” — dribble as far from them as you can.
  • The player who was called calls the next name. Get to this one — now they are saying names, not just hearing them.
  • The named player picks one of two directions and runs. Everyone else has to beat them over that line — with their ball.
  • Cones out. Everybody runs to that player’s position, not to the player. Straight into Find Your Position.
Open questions. Week one, half the team will freeze on the first call because they do not know the name yet — asking a neighbour out loud is the right answer, but say so before you start or they will stand there looking at you. — And ten players converging on one child is fun, and slightly alarming for that child. Stopping a few yards short might be a rule worth having, or might be the part they like best.

What You Shout

A name “Who is it?” “Heads up!”

“Who is it?” goes to the player who has stopped to look around, so somebody tells them. That is the drill working, not the drill failing.

Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

Everyone at once “Nine kids are not waiting their turn.”

A name game where players take turns has one person talking and nine listening. Here the whole team does something at the mention of a name — and you have to find that face in a moving crowd to obey the call.

The quiet second thing “Heads up.”

It is dribbling with the head up, taught sideways while they think they are playing a name game.
