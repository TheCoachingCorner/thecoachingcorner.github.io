Pass Out, Pass It In, Shoot

Serves · Lesson 14 — Shooting Mechanics, Lesson 10 — Passing Fundamentals (no page yet) 10 minutes ten, five a side

Needs · a goal, a goalie, a pile of balls beside each post, two cones for the wide spots

passingshooting

The big idea

PASS IT OUT → PASS IT IN → SHOOT

Quick, simple, lots of repetitions. A new one every ten or fifteen seconds.

1

Set Up

Two stations, mirrored, both shooting at the same goal. A line beside each post, a cone out wide on each side about level with the top of the big box, and one goalie.

Keep the balls next to the goal, where the passer stands, so the next rep can start the moment the last one ends.

2

How It Runs

  1. Pass it out. The player on the goal line passes wide.
  2. Pass it in. The wide player receives, one touch, then passes it across the face of the goal.
  3. Shoot. The shooter runs onto it and hits it, first time if they can.

The two sides alternate. Left goes, then right — one ball live at a time, so there is one goalie and nobody is shot at from behind.

A new rep every ten to fifteen seconds. As soon as the shot happens, the next player goes.

3

Rotation

1Passer
becomes the wide player
2Wide player
becomes the shooter
3Shooter
collects the balls, then passes

Everybody does all three jobs, and the collecting is what refills the pile by the goal.

4

Modifiers

Easier
  • The wide player may take two touches.
  • The shooter may take a touch before shooting.
  • No goalie.
Harder
  • One touch only for the wide player and the shooter.
  • Move the cone wider, so the pass in is longer.
  • Add a defender who starts on the goal line and chases the shooter.
  • The shooter has to call for it before the wide player plays it.
5

What You Shout

“In front of them!”“First time!” “Pass it in!”“Which post?”

“In front of them” is the one that matters. The pass across has to lead the shooter, not sit still.

6

What Did We Learn

At the water break, not on the grass.

  • Which passes were easy to shoot and which were hard? What was different?
  • Wide players — where did the ball need to end up?
  • Did the first-time shots go in more or less than the ones you took a touch on?
  • What did the goalie see that made it hard?
  • Saturday: the ball comes across the front of their goal. Where should you be running?
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

The same shot every time, arriving differently

A shooting drill with a stationary ball teaches the strike. This one teaches the strike on a ball already moving across them, which is what almost every real chance looks like.

The pass in is the part nobody practises

A ball that sits still in front of the shooter is a bad pass here even though it arrived. In front of them is the cue, and the wide player learns it by watching the shot that came off their pass.

Two stations means nobody queues

Five a line, three jobs, a rep every ten or fifteen seconds — which works out to standing still for well under a minute at a time.

The collecting is built into the rotation

The shooter fetches and becomes the next passer, so the pile by the goal refills itself and the drill never stops for balls.

Alternating the sides is what keeps it safe

Two balls live at one goal means somebody takes one in the back while facing the other way. Left, then right, and there is only ever one.

Open Questions

  • Whether first-time shooting is too much to ask at 10U. First time if possible is the right hedge, but it may be worth one round where everybody takes a touch and one where nobody does.
  • How wide the cone goes. Level with the top of the big box is the starting point; further out makes a better pass and a harder one for the shooter.
  • Whether one goalie is enough for ten kids alternating. They face a shot every ten seconds, which is more work than anybody else in the drill.
  • Whether the two stations should ever run at once. Faster, and the reason not to is the one above.
