2v2 To Goal

Serves · Lesson 04 — Defense Partners (the recovering half — teach the shape with 2v1 Defense Partners first), Lesson 14 — Shooting Mechanics 12 minutes five at a time

Needs · a goal, the big box, a ball, four bibs in two colours, a cone at the top of the box

passingshootingdefense

The big idea

TWO PASSES → THEN SHOOT

Both defenders start off the field and a long way from the ball. The first pass lets them on, so they arrive separated — which is what makes them splittable.

1

Set Up

Play in the big box and the ground just outside it. Everything starts from one side — call it the left, and run it from the right another time.

ACentral
outside the box, with the ball
AWide left
level with the top of the box
DBehind the goal line
to the left of the goal
DOff the right touchline
the far side from the ball
2

The Rule That Makes It Work

Defenders stay off the field until the first pass is played.

Two passes before you can shoot. Count them out loud.

“One! Two!” — then the goal is live. Everybody hears the count, so nobody has to referee it. A shot before two passes does not count, even if it goes in. The same first pass does both jobs: it starts the count and it releases the defenders.

3

How It Runs

  1. The central attacker plays the first pass. That is the whistle — both defenders come on.
  2. One more pass, then shoot.
  3. The rep ends on a goal, the goalie holding it, the ball leaving the area, or the defenders dribbling it out past the top of the box.
  4. Rotate one spot every time, whatever happened. Attackers become defenders.
  5. When everyone has played all four spots, the goalie swaps out.

Keep them short. If nothing has happened in about fifteen seconds, call it and reset — a 2v2 that has turned into a wrestling match is not teaching anybody anything.

4

Version 2 — In Your Face

Same drill, one change: the defenders start close, just off the attackers, and come at them the moment the ball moves. Now there is no time on the ball — and the two of them arriving together leave a gap between them.

A goal from a pass played between the two defenders is worth two.

That is the same gap lesson 04 spends its whole page telling defenders to close — here the attackers get paid for finding it, which is the fastest way for both sides to learn what it costs.

5

Modifiers

Easier
  • One pass before a shot.
  • Defenders start on the field, in normal positions.
  • Let them on at the first touch rather than the first pass.
  • No goalie. Shoot into an empty goal.
Harder
  • Three passes before a shot.
  • Defenders may not tackle for the first three seconds — only get in the way.
  • The defenders' way to win it is to dribble it out, not kick it out.
  • A shot only counts if the second attacker touched it last.
More than five
  • Two grids, two goals — needs a second adult. Six to ten is two groups of five.
  • Or the queue version off one goal: after each rep, attackers become defenders, defenders come off, and the next two go on. Nobody plays all four spots in one go, but nobody stands for more than a rep either.
6

What You Shout

“Count them out!”“One goes, one covers!” “Which post?”“Pass it in!”
7

What Did We Learn

At the water break, not on the grass.

  • When the ball moved, who went to it? How did you decide — or did you both go?
  • What happened the time you both went?
  • Defenders — was it the first pass or the second one that hurt you?
  • Attackers — where was the easy shot coming from?
  • Saturday: two of them break on you and you are the one who got back first. What is your job?
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

The defenders start out of position on purpose

A tidy 2v2 where everyone is already where they should be never produces the moment lesson 04 is about. Starting both defenders off the field means the attack is always moving before anybody can touch it, and the first thing that happens is the question who goes? — asked at a run. Getting it wrong is visible immediately, because the attack goes straight through the gap they left.

The first pass is the whistle

One event starts the count and releases the defenders, so there is nothing to call and nothing to argue about. It also guarantees the attack gets going — a 2v2 that is contested from the first touch usually dies before a second attacker is involved.

The pass requirement makes the second attacker exist

Without it, a 2v2 at this age is one child dribbling and one child watching. Two passes forces the ball to travel, which forces the other attacker to be somewhere useful — which is the whole thing being taught.

Counting out loud is what makes it self-refereed

One coach cannot watch the pass count and the defending and the finish. Ten children can count to two, and they will correct each other faster and louder than any adult.

Everyone plays every spot

The child who always defends finds out what an attacker sees, and the one who always attacks finds out how it feels to be the covering defender with somebody bearing down. Rotating every rep also stops it becoming a contest between the same two kids.

The goalie swaps on a full rotation, not on a whim

Keeping goal is a turn, not a sentence, and there is a countable moment when it ends.

Open Questions

  • Two passes or three. Two is the starting number because three under pressure at 10U tends to end the rep before a shot ever happens. If they are getting to two easily, go up.
  • How far off the field the defenders start. Right on the line is a sprint they can win; five yards back is a chance the attack always converts. Start them on the line and move them back only if the attack never gets a shot away.
  • Whether the behind-the-goal defender can ever get back in time. They are the furthest from everything. If they are never a factor, that spot is a rest rather than a rep — move them to the other touchline.
  • Whether the goalie should count as a passing option for the defenders once they win it. It would tie this to lesson 13, and it would also make a short drill longer.
  • Five players is a station, not a practice. Two grids needs two adults, and that is the real constraint on running this with a full team.
