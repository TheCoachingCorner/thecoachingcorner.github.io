One Way Out

Serves · Lesson 03 — One on One Defense, the pressure and turn them beats 10 minutes Any even number

Needs · a channel about 12 × 8 yds · 4 cones for two gates · a ball per pair

introdefense

The big idea

DON’T TACKLE → STEER

Two gates, one of them closed. Make them use the other.

Set Up

A channel with two gates at the defender’s end, one each side. Attacker with a ball at the far end, defender starting in the middle.

How It Runs

The rule that makes it safe

Say it first

“The defender may not tackle. At all.”

No stabbing, no lunging, no going to ground. The only tool is where you stand.

The drill

Whole team10 min
  1. Coach names a gate: that one is closed.
  2. The attacker tries to get through the closed gate. The defender makes them use the other one.
  3. Through the closed gate → attacker’s point. Through the open one, or stopped for 6 seconds → defender’s point.
  4. Swap every rep. Play to 3, then find a new partner.

Round 1 walking. Round 2 the defender may jog. Round 3 live. Run two or three channels side by side so nobody is standing and watching.

Modifiers

Easier
  • Wider gap between the gates — more room to be wrong in.
  • Walking only, every round.
  • Defender starts closer to the gates.
Harder
  • Narrow the channel, so getting side-on matters more.
  • The defender picks the closed gate and does not say which. The attacker has to read it.
  • Once the attacker has committed to a gate, now the defender may win the ball.

What You Shout

“Which gate is closed?” “Show them the open one!” “Stop short!”
Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

Why no tackling “The only tool is where you stand.”

It removes every way a nine-year-old gets hurt in a 1v1, and it is the only way they learn the angle. A child allowed to tackle will always tackle, and never finds out that standing in the right place would have done it for them.

Why two gates, not a goal “Make them go left” beats “stop them”.

A defender told to stop someone tries to win the ball. A defender told to send them one way has to solve it with their body, which is what turn them is. The gates score the thing being taught rather than the thing that is easy to count.

The mistake shows itself Through the closed gate means you were square.

You gave them both ways. Nobody has to be told.

Open questions. When to allow tackling: never means they never practise the last part, too early means they go back to stabbing, and letting it in only after the attacker commits is the guess. — And gate width: too narrow and the defender wins by standing still. 2 yds is a starting point, untested.
