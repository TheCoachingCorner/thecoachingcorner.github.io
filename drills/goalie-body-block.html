Goalie Body Block

Serves · Lesson 02 — Goalie Introduction 10 minutes Any even number

Needs · a ball per pair · two lines ten feet apart

introgoalkeeping

The big idea

HANDS MIGHT MISS → THE BODY IS THE BACKUP

Get behind the ball first. Then the hands only have to hold it.

Set Up

Two lines facing each other, ten feet apart, everyone opposite a partner. One ball per pair.

Ten feet is close, and it has to be. From twenty feet away they have time to stroll across; the whole point is getting the body over in time.

How It Runs

In pairs

Everyone at once10 min
  1. Roll the ball to your partner — one or two steps to their left, not at their feet.
  2. They get their body in front of the ball, scoop it up, and hug it.
  3. They roll or kick it back the same way, to your side.
  4. Next one goes to the right. Keep alternating.

“Feet first. Then hug it.”

Two hands, and the ball finishes against the chest. A ball trapped between the hands and the ground is caught; a ball that goes under a diving hand was never caught.

Easier / Harder

Easier
  • Straight at them for the first minute, just to get the hug right.
  • Five feet apart, so nobody is beaten by the distance.
  • On their knees — body behind it only, no footwork.
Harder
  • Roll it further to the side, so they shuffle two or three steps.
  • Bounce it instead of rolling it.
  • Don’t say which side it is going to.
Open questions. How hard is too hard: the drill gets better the firmer the ball is struck and worse the moment one child is frightened of it, and that line is different for every pair. Letting the pairs set it themselves is probably right. — And whether to run it in front of a real goal, which adds the fear of conceding. That is either the point or exactly the thing to keep out. Untested.

What You Shout

“Feet first!” “Hug it!” “Behind it!”

“Behind it!” goes to the player who reached across instead of moving across — the exact mistake this drill exists to catch.

Why it works Everything above is what you need on the grass. Below is the thinking behind it — read it once before the session, not during.

Why It Works

Why the body “Your hands might miss. Your body is your backup.”

Reach sideways with hands alone and there is one thing between the ball and the goal. Shuffle across first and there are two — and the second one is much bigger.

Why to the side “Never at their feet.”

Straight at them, they never have to move, and the drill teaches nothing.
